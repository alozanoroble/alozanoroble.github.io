AI and Mathematics: A Timeline

Milestones in AI and mathematics, newest first. Each event links to one source about it. Many of the most recent claims are still being checked by the mathematical community.

2026

  1. Nov 20, 2026

    Two claimed proofs of the Riemann Hypothesis

    Levent Alpöge (Anthropic) announces a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis and GRH found with Claude and written in Lean; the Lean FRO and Mathlib Initiative vouch for the formal proof the next day. The same day, Alessandro Maria Masullo posts a claimed human proof of GRH.

    New mathematicsFormalizationMy RH updates page
  2. Nov 20, 2026

    Navier–Stokes and Euler blowup by Buckmaster and Alpöge

    Two months after OpenAI’s result for the forced equations, Tristan Buckmaster and Levent Alpöge post an AI-aided proof, with a Lean formalization, that the Navier–Stokes equations can blow up in finite time with no outside force. The same day, with Matei Coiculescu, they release a 55-page paper proving blowup for the Euler equations. Independent review is pending.

    New mathematicsFormalizationScientific American
  3. Nov 9, 2026

    Fermat’s Last Theorem formalized in 30 hours

    Anthropic reports that a team of 1,000 Claude Fable 5.2 agents fully formalized Fermat’s Last Theorem in Lean in 30 hours. The first formalization, in September, took an unreleased research model 11 days with human guidance.

    FormalizationAnthropic
  4. Oct 27, 2026

    Axiom formalizes the Classification of Finite Simple Groups

    Axiom announces a complete Lean formalization of the Classification of Finite Simple Groups, the “Enormous Theorem” whose human proof runs to some 15,000 pages, produced by swarms of AI agents directed by mathematicians.

    FormalizationAxiom
  5. Oct 26, 2026

    OpenAI announces three discoveries, and controversy follows

    OpenAI announces three AI-produced results: the local smoothing conjecture in all dimensions (which implies the Kakeya and Stein restriction conjectures), a disproof of the Exponential Time Hypothesis, and an existence result for Yang–Mills theory. Anthropic announces similar results hours later. Released ahead of outside review, the claims draw strong criticism from mathematicians.

    New mathematicsScientific American
  6. Oct 6, 2026

    OpenAI releases 700+ solutions to open problems

    OpenAI posts 722 unreviewed AI-generated manuscripts at once, claiming progress on hundreds of open problems. Three are withdrawn within days after a sign error, and many mathematicians object to the release.

    New mathematicsNature
  7. Oct 2, 2026

    Meta’s Muse Spark helps solve six problems

    Meta reports that mathematicians working with Muse Spark, through its ordinary chat interface, produced six papers, five of them answering previously open questions. Three of the results were also found independently by other groups.

    Human–AI collaborationNew mathematicsMeta AI Research
  8. Sep 8, 2026

    Navier–Stokes blowup by OpenAI; Euler blowup by Buckmaster and Alpöge

    OpenAI announces that an internal model proved finite-time blowup for the Navier–Stokes equations, with a Lean formalization. Tristan Buckmaster and Levent Alpöge, who had obtained blowup for the Euler equations, dispute the credit.

    New mathematicsFormalizationQuanta Magazine
  9. Sep 4, 2026

    Claude formalizes Fermat’s Last Theorem

    Anthropic announces that teams of Claude agents produced the first complete computer-checked proof of Fermat’s Last Theorem in Lean, about 13 million lines written in 11 days and reviewed by Kevin Buzzard.

    FormalizationAnthropic
  10. Sep 3, 2026

    Small gaps between primes: 186

    OpenAI’s Astra model lowers the bound for small gaps between primes: infinitely many pairs of consecutive primes differ by at most 186, down from 246. The Lean formalization takes some deep known results, such as Deligne’s bounds, as axioms.

    New mathematicsFormalizationScience News
  11. Aug 23, 2026

    A complex structure on the six-sphere

    Levent Alpöge announces, with a roughly 100-page paper written with Claude, that S⁶ has a complex structure, answering the Hopf problem. Checking the argument is ongoing, and several follow-up papers have appeared.

    New mathematicsHuman–AI collaborationScientific American
  12. Aug 23, 2026

    An elliptic curve of rank 31

    Three days after rank 30, Claude, Levent Alpöge and Ava Howell find an elliptic curve over ℚ of rank at least 31.

    New mathematicsDujella’s rank records
  13. Aug 20, 2026

    An elliptic curve of rank 30

    Claude, Levent Alpöge and Ava Howell find an elliptic curve over ℚ of rank at least 30, breaking the Elkies–Klagsbrun record of 29, after I “double-dared” Levent to find one.

    New mathematicsScientific American
  14. Aug 10, 2026

    67.2% of zeta zeros satisfy RH

    Asked to attack the Riemann Hypothesis, an unreleased Claude research model instead finds a new argument raising the known proportion of zeta zeros on the critical line from 41.6% to 67.2%, checked by mathematicians and formalized in Lean.

    New mathematicsFormalizationAnthropic
  15. Aug 9, 2026

    M₂₃ is a Galois group over ℚ

    Huang, Jackson, Lee, Poonen, Pries and Zhang realize the Mathieu group M₂₃, the last sporadic group left, as a Galois group over ℚ. Claude and ChatGPT sped up much of the computation.

    Human–AI collaborationNew mathematicsarXiv
  16. Aug 4, 2026

    The period-index conjecture is false

    Alexander Perry disproves the period-index conjecture. He credits a flawed example suggested by ChatGPT with inspiring the construction.

    Human–AI collaborationNew mathematicsarXiv
  17. Aug 1, 2026

    OpenAI’s “Astra” releases ten advances

    OpenAI releases ten research-level results in mathematics and theoretical computer science, including non-sofic groups, sphere packing, Ramsey numbers and Ehrhart’s conjecture, with arguments generated by the model and converted to Lean certificates.

    New mathematicsFormalizationOpenAI
  18. July 2026

    A counterexample to the Jacobian conjecture

    Levent Alpöge reports that Claude helped find a remarkably small counterexample to the Jacobian conjecture in dimension 3 and above, since checked by independent mathematicians. The two-dimensional case remains open.

    New mathematicsHuman–AI collaborationScienceDaily
  19. June 2026

    ChatGPT finds a fine-grained complexity result

    Saha, Xu and Ye report that the original proof of essentially quadratic hardness for Furthest Pair, under SETH, was found by ChatGPT 5.5 Pro and then checked and edited by the authors.

    New mathematicsHuman–AI collaborationarXiv
  20. May 2026

    Human mathematics builds on the AI result

    Will Sawin quickly makes the unit-distance counterexample quantitative, with more than n^1.014 unit distances, and other papers reuse the techniques of the AI proof.

    Human–AI collaborationarXiv
  21. May 2026

    Erdős’s unit-distance conjecture is disproved

    An OpenAI model constructs planar point sets with n^(1+δ) unit distances, disproving a decades-old conjecture of Erdős. Noga Alon, Tim Gowers and others digest and verify the argument.

    New mathematicsOpenAI
  22. Jan 2026

    Gemini and the Erdős problems

    Researchers run Gemini on about 700 open problems in the Erdős Problems database and report five apparently new solutions, plus eight problems whose solutions already existed in obscure literature.

    New mathematicsHuman–AI collaborationarXiv

2025

  1. July 2025

    Gemini Deep Think: IMO gold

    An advanced Gemini Deep Think model scores at gold-medal level at the 2025 International Mathematical Olympiad, working end to end in natural language within the time limit.

    Reasoning milestoneGoogle DeepMind
  2. May 2025

    AlphaEvolve

    Google DeepMind couples Gemini with evolutionary search and automatic evaluators, finding new matrix-multiplication algorithms and new bounds for several mathematical search problems.

    New mathematicsGoogle DeepMind
  3. Apr 2025

    DeepSeek-Prover-V2

    An open-weight Lean prover combining informal reasoning, subgoal decomposition and reinforcement learning reaches 88.9% on miniF2F and solves 49 PutnamBench problems.

    FormalizationarXiv

2024

  1. July 2024

    AlphaProof and AlphaGeometry 2: IMO silver

    Google DeepMind’s systems solve four of six problems at the 2024 IMO, silver-medal level, with AlphaProof searching for proofs in Lean using reinforcement learning.

    Reasoning milestoneFormalizationGoogle DeepMind
  2. Jan 2024

    AlphaGeometry

    DeepMind’s neuro-symbolic AlphaGeometry solves 25 of 30 Olympiad geometry problems, close to gold-medalist performance, with machine-checkable symbolic deductions.

    Reasoning milestoneGoogle DeepMind

2023

  1. Dec 2023

    FunSearch

    DeepMind combines an LLM with evolutionary search to find new cap-set constructions, improving a known lower bound: an LLM-generated program produces publishable new mathematics.

    New mathematicsNature
  2. June 2023

    LeanDojo and ReProver

    An open environment, dataset and retrieval-augmented prover for LLM theorem proving in Lean and mathlib, making this research far more reproducible.

    FormalizationarXiv

2022

  1. Oct 2022

    AlphaTensor

    DeepMind uses reinforcement learning to discover matrix-multiplication algorithms that match or beat the best known human constructions for some sizes.

    New mathematicsNature
  2. 2022

    Autoformalization with LLMs

    Wu, Jiang, Li and collaborators show that LLMs can translate informal mathematics into formal statements, and use the resulting data to improve theorem proving on miniF2F.

    FormalizationNeurIPS

2021

  1. Dec 2021

    DeepMind works with mathematicians

    Neural networks help mathematicians spot patterns in knot theory and representation theory, guiding them to new conjectures and theorems.

    Human–AI collaborationNature
  2. 2021

    miniF2F

    A benchmark of 488 Olympiad and undergraduate problems formalized in Lean, Metamath and other systems, which drives the next years of neural theorem proving.

    FormalizationarXiv

2020

  1. Sep 2020

    GPT-f

    OpenAI’s GPT-f proves theorems in Metamath and finds shorter proofs that are adopted into the main Metamath library, the first deep-learning proofs accepted by a formal-mathematics community.

    FormalizationarXiv

2019

  1. 2017–2019

    Lean and mathlib

    The community builds mathlib, a large unified library of machine-checkable mathematics in Lean, and the IMO Grand Challenge sets the goal of an AI that wins IMO gold with verified proofs.

    FormalizationarXiv
